## 1. Core trade‑off: Is the refreshed XM4 worth the upgrade?  

If you already own the 2018 Sony WH‑1000XM4, the decision rests on measurable improvements in driver design, active‑noise‑cancellation (ANC) processing, and battery life versus the higher price tag. The refreshed model adds a 2‑dB increase in laboratory‑measured ANC (from 32 dB to 34 dB at 250 Hz), a 10 % extension of battery life (30 h → 33 h under the same test conditions), and a latency reduction of roughly 7 ms in aptX Adaptive mode. These changes are modest but quantifiable. For first‑time buyers of premium over‑ear headphones, the refreshed XM4 provides the latest iteration of Sony’s flagship ANC platform at a price that still undercuts the Apple AirPods Max and matches the Bose 700 in the United States.

## 3. Sound, ANC, and latency performance  

Because the refreshed XM4 has been on the market for only a few weeks, the pool of independent measurements is still limited. The most widely cited sources as of September 2024 are RTINGS.com, SoundGuys, and the Audio Science Review (ASR) lab. The table below summarises the publicly available data.

| Test | Source (date) | Original XM4 | Refreshed XM4 | Observed change |
|------|---------------|--------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Frequency response (averaged)** | RTINGS.com (Sep 2024) | 4 Hz – 40 kHz, ±3 dB | 4 Hz – 40 kHz, ±2.5 dB | Tighter tolerance in the 2–5 kHz region; inaudible for most listeners. |
| **Total Harmonic Distortion @ 1 kHz, 100 % volume** | SoundGuys (Aug 2024) | 0.08 % | 0.07 % | 0.01 % reduction, well below the audible threshold. |
| **Active Noise Cancellation (A‑weighted) – laboratory** | Audio Science Review (July 2024) | 32 dB reduction (steady‑tone 250 Hz) | 34 dB reduction (same tone) | ~2 dB improvement, most noticeable in low‑mid frequencies such as airplane cabin noise. |
| **Passive isolation (no ANC)** | User‑collected SPL measurements (various) | 22 dB (average) | 22 dB (average) | No change; earcup geometry unchanged. |
| **Latency (aptX Adaptive, 1080p video)** | RTINGS.com (Sep 2024) | 115 ms | 108 ms | ~7 ms reduction; may benefit fast‑action gaming or video sync. |
| **Battery‑life test (ANC on, 90 % volume)** | SoundGuys (Aug 2024) | 30 h | 33 h | 3‑hour extension confirmed under identical test conditions. |

**How to verify** – All three labs publish full PDFs of their methodology. If you own a pair, a simple ANC test can be performed with a calibrated SPL meter (or a calibrated smartphone app) by measuring the attenuation at 250 Hz with and without ANC. For latency, the free Android app “Bluetooth Audio Latency Test” provides a rough figure; laboratory equipment remains more precise.

Overall, the refreshed XM4 delivers incremental but measurable gains in ANC, battery life, and latency while preserving the original’s warm, slightly bass‑forward sound signature. Listeners who already favor that tuning should find the new model familiar.

A 2–4 dB difference in ANC translates to roughly a 20–30 % reduction in perceived low‑frequency noise, according to acoustic research from the Acoustical Society of America. In practical terms, the refreshed XM4 should feel noticeably quieter in environments dominated by engine rumble or HVAC noise. Battery life is also a clear differentiator: the XM4’s 33 h exceeds the Bose 700 and AirPods Max by 13 h and 13 h respectively under comparable test conditions.

When weighing a purchase, consider the following scenarios:

* **Prioritising ANC and endurance** – The XM4’s 34 dB ANC and 33‑hour runtime give it a measurable advantage over the Bose 700 and AirPods Max, especially for long flights or multi‑day trips.  
* **Ecosystem integration** – The AirPods Max offers seamless spatial audio and automatic device switching within Apple’s ecosystem, features that Sony does not replicate.  
* **Codec flexibility** – Sony’s support for LDAC and the newer LC3 codec benefits Android users and high‑resolution streaming enthusiasts; the Bose 700 and AirPods Max are limited to SBC/AAC and Apple’s proprietary codecs respectively.
