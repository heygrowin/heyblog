If you need a single tool that can store rich notes **and** keep a reliable to‑do list, the decision today is to adopt **Notion** as your primary workspace. It gives you a unified database, cross‑device syncing and enough customisation to replace a notebook, a task manager and, for many users, a lightweight project‑tracking system. Anything else will either force you to juggle separate apps or sacrifice flexibility you’ll likely miss later.

## Why a single app beats two specialised apps  

### Integration saves mental bandwidth  
When you write a meeting summary in one programme and then open another to add the action items, you create a split‑attention loop. Every time you switch you risk losing context: the exact wording of a decision, a link you bookmarked, or the deadline you set. A single app lets you embed a task directly beneath a note, tag it, and see the status change without leaving the page. Over a week, that reduction in context‑switching can add up to a noticeable boost in productivity, especially for knowledge workers who spend most of their day reading and writing.

### Reducing duplication of data  
Separate apps often store the same piece of information twice – for example, a project brief in Evernote and a checklist of deliverables in Todoist. When you edit the brief, you must remember to update the checklist manually. Notion’s relational database lets you link a note to a task, so a change in one place propagates automatically. This eliminates the need for manual synchronisation and reduces the chance of outdated information persisting in your workflow.

### Risks and limits of “all‑in‑one”  
The trade‑off is that a highly customisable platform can feel overwhelming at first. Notion’s block‑based editor, while powerful, requires an initial learning curve. If you need a truly offline‑first experience – for example, when you travel to locations with poor internet – Notion’s desktop app does cache pages but does not guarantee full offline editing for every block type. In those edge cases you may still need a secondary, lightweight note‑taking app such as Apple Notes or a plain‑text editor.

## How Notion meets the core criteria  

### Note‑taking features  

* **Rich media support** – You can embed PDFs, images, videos, code snippets and web bookmarks directly into a page.  
* **Hierarchical organisation** – Pages can contain sub‑pages, allowing you to mirror a folder structure without losing the ability to link across the hierarchy.  
* **Searchability** – Notion indexes the full text of every block, so a search for a phrase will surface notes, tasks and even table entries at once.  

### Task‑management features  

* **Database views** – A single “Tasks” table can be displayed as a list, a Kanban board, a calendar or a timeline, depending on the project stage.  
* **Relations and roll‑ups** – You can link a task to a note, then roll up a property such as “Due date” onto the note page, giving you a quick visual cue of pending actions.  
* **Reminders and integrations** – Built‑in date reminders push notifications to your desktop or phone, and the app can sync with Google Calendar via a simple integration.  

### Cross‑platform availability  

Notion runs natively on Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and has a web version. All changes are stored in the cloud and sync within seconds. For users who prefer a desktop‑only environment, the Windows and macOS apps can be set to launch at login, providing a seamless experience across work and home computers.

### Pricing and data ownership  

* **Free tier** – Allows unlimited pages and blocks for individuals, with a 5 MB file upload limit per file. This is sufficient for most personal note‑taking and task‑tracking needs.  
* **Personal Pro** – At a modest monthly price (check Notion’s current pricing page for the exact figure), you unlock unlimited file uploads and version history.  
* **Export options** – Notion lets you export a workspace as HTML, Markdown or CSV, giving you a fallback if you ever need to migrate data.  

Because the pricing is transparent and the export function is built‑in, you can verify the cost‑benefit ratio yourself by signing up for the free plan, using the app for a week, and then checking the “Settings → Plans” page for the exact current price.

## How to test it before committing  

1. **Create a free Notion account** – Use your email address; no credit‑card details are required.  
2. **Import a recent project** – Drag a folder of PDFs, Word documents and a simple CSV of tasks into a new workspace. Notion will automatically create pages for each file.  
3. **Build a task view** – Add a “Tasks” table, set the “Due date” property, and create a Kanban board view. Link a few tasks to the imported notes using the “Relation” property.  
4. **Set a reminder** – Choose a date and time, then watch for the notification on your phone or desktop.  
5. **Test offline behaviour** – Disconnect from the internet, open a page you edited earlier, make a change, then reconnect. Verify that the change syncs without error.  
6. **Export a sample** – Go to “Settings & Members → Export all workspace content” and choose Markdown. Open the exported files in a plain‑text editor to confirm the data is readable.  

Spend at least two days using the setup for everyday tasks – meeting notes, quick to‑dos and occasional brainstorming. If you encounter a blocker that you cannot resolve through Notion’s help centre or community forums, note it; that will be the most honest indicator of whether the tool truly fits your needs.

## What to do about it  

The decisive step is to adopt Notion as your primary note‑taking and to‑do platform **today**. Sign up for the free tier, import a current project, and create a simple task database linked to your notes. Use the built‑in reminder and calendar view for the next week; if the workflow feels smoother and you no longer need to toggle between separate apps, upgrade to the Personal Pro plan for unlimited file uploads. Should you discover a critical offline limitation, keep a lightweight plain‑text editor as a fallback, but let Notion remain the central hub for all new information. This approach gives you a single source of truth, measurable productivity gains and a clear path to scale as your workload evolves.
