If you want a budgeting app that works outside the Microsoft 365 ecosystem, respects data portability and keeps costs predictable, the decision today is to **skip Copilot Budget and adopt a dedicated personal‑finance tool such as MoneyHub, YNAB or Emma**.  Copilot Budget’s main advantage—AI‑driven insights built into the Microsoft stack—does not outweigh its lock‑in, limited export options and the extra subscription you already pay for Microsoft 365.  The alternatives listed below give you comparable tracking features, clearer pricing and full control over your financial data.

## Why Copilot Budget falls short for most users

### AI assistance is a nice‑to‑have, not a must‑have

Copilot Budget markets itself on “AI‑generated spending insights”. In practice the suggestions are derived from the same transaction categorisation that any decent budgeting app already provides. If you are comfortable reviewing your own statements, the AI layer adds little value and can even misclassify niche expenses (e.g., freelance invoicing or foreign‑currency purchases).  

### Vendor lock‑in and data export limits

Copilot Budget stores data in a proprietary Microsoft cloud format. Export is limited to a CSV file that omits custom tags and recurring‑transaction rules. To verify the limitation, open the app’s settings, navigate to **Data → Export**, and you will see only “Download CSV”. There is no API for third‑party tools, meaning you cannot automatically sync the data with other services such as accounting software or a personal data‑warehouse.

### Pricing adds to an already‑paid Microsoft 365 bill

Copilot Budget is offered as an add‑on to Microsoft 365 at roughly £5 per month (the exact figure varies by region and plan). If you already pay £12–£15 per month for Microsoft 365, the extra charge may be hard to justify when a standalone app can be found for £3–£4 per month, or even free with a modest feature set.

### Platform coverage is uneven

The app is primarily a web‑based interface optimised for Windows and Edge. Mobile apps exist for iOS and Android, but they lag behind in feature parity and receive updates less frequently than the desktop version. Users who rely on a consistent experience across devices will notice missing visualisations and delayed syncs.

### MoneyHub – the most balanced choice

MoneyHub provides a clean dashboard, automatic bank feeds via Plaid, and the ability to export data in both CSV and OFX formats. Its free tier allows unlimited accounts and basic reporting; the premium tier (£3 per month) unlocks custom tags and cash‑flow forecasting. Because MoneyHub stores data in a UK‑based data centre, you can verify compliance by checking the **Data‑Protection** page on their website, which links to the ICO registration number.

### YNAB – the budgeting‑first philosophy

YNAB does not claim AI insights; instead, it enforces a “zero‑based budgeting” rule that forces you to allocate every pound before the month ends. The learning curve is steeper, but the method has been shown in independent studies to improve savings rates. YNAB’s API lets you pull transaction data into tools like Excel or Power BI, giving you full control over analysis. Pricing is £5 per month, with a 34‑day free trial.

### Emma – the lightweight, ad‑supported option

Emma’s free tier displays all linked accounts, categorises spend automatically, and sends real‑time alerts when you exceed a set limit (e.g., £200 on dining out). The premium tier (£2.99 per month) removes ads and adds custom categories. While Emma lacks deep reporting, it is sufficient for users who want a quick overview without committing to a full‑featured budgeting system.

## Practical steps to migrate away from Copilot Budget

1. **Export your existing data** – In Copilot Budget, go to Settings → Data → Export and download the CSV file.  
2. **Clean the CSV** – Open the file in a spreadsheet program and remove any columns that contain only “‑”. Add a column for “Tag” if you plan to use custom tags in the new app.  
3. **Choose the target app** – Based on the matrix above, decide whether MoneyHub, YNAB or Emma best matches your weighted criteria.  
4. **Create an account** – Sign up for the free tier to test the import workflow before committing to a paid plan.  
5. **Import the cleaned CSV** – Each alternative offers an “Import” button in the settings. Follow the on‑screen mapping guide to align CSV columns with the app’s fields.  
6. **Re‑establish bank feeds** – Authorise the new app to connect to your banks via Plaid or the provider’s native API. Verify that the first automatic sync matches the balances you exported.  
7. **Set up custom categories** – If you use tags for “Freelance Income” or “Pet Expenses”, recreate them now. Most apps let you assign colours or icons for quick visual reference.  
8. **Run a parallel month** – Keep Copilot Budget active for one billing cycle while you use the new app. Compare the totals at month‑end to ensure no transactions were missed.  
9. **Cancel Copilot Budget** – Once you are confident the migration is complete, go to Microsoft 365 admin portal, locate the Copilot add‑on and turn it off to avoid further charges.

## What to do about it

The clear choice is to **move to a dedicated budgeting app that offers open data export, lower ongoing costs and full cross‑device sync**. MoneyHub provides the most balanced feature set for the price, while YNAB is ideal if you want a disciplined budgeting method, and Emma works for a lightweight, low‑cost solution. Use the decision matrix above to confirm which alternative aligns with your priorities, then follow the migration steps to transition without losing any historic data. Acting now prevents another month of paying for a service that limits your financial freedom.
