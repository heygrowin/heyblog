If you need a note‑taking app that can replace Evernote, the most sensible decision today is to adopt **Microsoft OneNote** – it offers comparable organisational depth, a free‑forever tier that matches Evernote’s core features, and tighter integration with the Microsoft 365 ecosystem that many users already rely on.

## Why Evernote is no longer the default choice

Evernote’s long‑standing reputation stems from its notebook hierarchy, powerful search, and web‑clipper. However, three practical issues have pushed it out of the default slot for many users:

1. **Pricing pressure** – Evernote’s free plan now caps monthly uploads at 60 MB and limits device sync to two devices. The premium tier is priced per user per month and can become costly for families or small teams.
2. **Feature drift** – Recent updates have focused on AI‑driven suggestions and premium‑only templates, while core note‑taking capabilities have seen fewer enhancements.
3. **Ecosystem lock‑in** – Evernote stores data in a proprietary format. Exporting large libraries into open formats (e.g., markdown or plain text) can be time‑consuming, making migration risky.

These factors do not mean Evernote is unusable, but they create a clear trade‑off: continue paying for a service that limits growth, or move to a platform that offers a broader free tier and better cross‑app compatibility.

## Defining the criteria for a replacement

Before committing to OneNote, confirm that it satisfies the criteria that matter to you. Below is a checklist you can use to audit any candidate app:

- **Storage limits** – Does the free tier provide enough monthly upload capacity for your workflow?
- **Device sync** – Are there limits on the number of devices that can be linked?
- **Organisation model** – Does the app support notebooks, tags, and hierarchical structures?
- **Search capability** – Can you search within PDFs, images (OCR), and handwritten notes?
- **Export options** – Are notes exportable to open formats (markdown, HTML, PDF) without loss of structure?
- **Collaboration tools** – Is real‑time sharing and commenting available, and under what plan?
- **Platform coverage** – Are Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, and web versions all fully featured?
- **Security and privacy** – What encryption is applied at rest and in transit? Does the provider publish a clear privacy policy?
- **Integration** – Does the app link to the tools you already use (e.g., Outlook, Teams, Google Drive)?

You can create a simple spreadsheet with these rows and score each app on a scale of 0‑5. The total will give you a quick visual of which option aligns best with your priorities.

### Why OneNote scores highest for most users

- **Unlimited free storage** – OneNote stores notes in OneDrive, which offers 5 GB of free space for a Microsoft account and can be expanded cheaply. This removes the upload caps that Evernote imposes.
- **No device limit** – You can sign in on as many phones, tablets, and computers as you like, a crucial factor for people who switch between work and personal devices.
- **Rich organisational model** – The three‑tier hierarchy (notebook → section → page) mirrors Evernote’s notebook‑note structure while allowing deeper nesting when needed.
- **Strong search** – Built‑in OCR indexes images and PDFs, making it easy to locate information without third‑party plug‑ins.
- **Export flexibility** – OneNote pages can be saved as PDF or exported to Word, and there are third‑party tools that convert them to markdown if you prefer plain‑text workflows.
- **Integration** – If you already use Outlook, Teams, or Word, OneNote links directly into those apps, reducing context‑switching.

## Decision framework: weighted scoring model

To move from a gut feeling to a defensible decision, apply a weighted scoring model. Here’s a step‑by‑step guide you can replicate in a spreadsheet:

1. **Assign weights** – Decide how important each criterion is to you (e.g., storage = 20 %, device sync = 15 %, search = 25 %, price = 10 %, integration = 15 %, security = 15 %). The total must equal 100 %.
2. **Score each app** – Rate each app from 0 (does not meet) to 5 (exceeds) for every criterion.
3. **Calculate weighted scores** – Multiply the score by the weight (expressed as a decimal). For example, if OneNote scores 5 on storage and storage weight is 0.20, the contribution is 1.0.
4. **Sum the rows** – The total gives a comparative figure; the highest total indicates the best overall fit.

## Migration considerations

Switching note‑taking platforms can be disruptive if you have a large existing library. Here are concrete steps to minimise friction:

1. **Export from Evernote** – Use Evernote’s built‑in export function to generate an ENEX file (XML format). This file contains all notes, tags, and attachments.
2. **Import into OneNote** – Microsoft provides a free “Importer” tool that reads ENEX files and recreates the notebook hierarchy in OneNote. Verify a small batch first to ensure formatting is preserved.
3. **Check attachments** – Images and PDFs usually transfer intact, but hand‑written notes may lose OCR metadata. Run a quick search in OneNote after import to confirm they are indexed.
4. **Re‑tag and reorganise** – OneNote uses tags differently (e.g., “Important”, “Question”). Map your Evernote tags to OneNote equivalents during the import process, or create a master tag list afterwards.
5. **Set up sync** – Sign in to OneDrive on each device, enable OneNote sync, and confirm that changes appear across all platforms within a few minutes.
6. **Backup** – Keep a copy of the original ENEX file and a local export of OneNote (File → Export → OneNote package) until you are confident the migration is complete.

If you prefer an open‑source route, Joplin can also import ENEX files directly, though you will lose Microsoft‑specific formatting. Test with a handful of notes before committing.

## What to do about it

- **Decision**: Choose Microsoft OneNote as your primary Evernote replacement.
- **Immediate action**: Sign up for a free Microsoft account, install OneNote on your main devices, and run the Evernote Importer on a test notebook.
- **Evaluation period**: Use OneNote for two weeks, focusing on storage, search, and cross‑device sync. If any critical gap appears, revisit the weighted scoring model and consider Joplin or Notion as secondary options.
- **Long‑term**: Keep the ENEX backup for at least six months, then archive it securely. Periodically review your note‑taking workflow to ensure the chosen app still meets evolving needs.
