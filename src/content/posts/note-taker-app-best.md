**Recommendation:** If you need a note‑taking app that can grow from quick daily jottings to a full‑blown knowledge base, the safest decision today is to adopt **Notion** as your primary tool, and keep a lightweight backup app such as **Apple Notes** (or **Google Keep** on Android) for ultra‑quick capture.  

Notion offers the most flexible structure, reliable cross‑platform sync, and a free tier that is generous enough for most personal and small‑team workflows. It does require a modest learning curve, but the long‑term organisational payoff outweighs the initial time investment for anyone who wants more than a simple list of text snippets.

---

## The core trade‑off: flexibility versus simplicity  

| Aspect | Notion (recommended) | Evernote | Microsoft OneNote | Apple Notes / Google Keep |
|--------|----------------------|----------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Structure** | Pages, databases, nested tables, kanban boards | Linear notebooks, tags | Section‑page hierarchy, tags | Plain notes, limited nesting |
| **Learning curve** | Moderate – needs a few hours to set up templates | Low – familiar UI | Low‑moderate – Office‑style layout | Very low |
| **Cross‑device sync** | Real‑time, web‑based, desktop, iOS, Android | Near‑real‑time, but occasional lag | Real‑time via Microsoft cloud | Real‑time, but limited to ecosystem |
| **Offline access** | Full offline editing (desktop & mobile) | Limited offline (premium) | Good offline on desktop, mobile varies | Full offline |
| **Export / portability** | Markdown, CSV, PDF, HTML; API for custom export | ENEX, HTML, PDF; limited API | OneNote format, PDF; no bulk export | Apple Notes → Mail, no bulk export |
| **Collaboration** | Real‑time editing, comments, permissions | Shared notebooks, slower sync | Shared notebooks, Office integration | Shared notes via iCloud/Google |
| **Free tier limits** | Unlimited pages, up to 5 MB file upload, 1 GB total storage | 60 MB monthly upload limit | Unlimited pages, 5 GB OneDrive storage | Unlimited within ecosystem |
| **Pricing for upgrades** | $8 / month (Personal Pro) for unlimited uploads | $7.99 / month (Premium) for more upload | $69.99 / year (Microsoft 365) for extra features | Free (no paid tier) |

The decision hinges on whether you value **customisable organisation** (databases, linked views, templates) more than **instant, no‑frills capture**. Notion sits at the flexible end; Apple Notes/Google Keep sit at the simple end. Most users eventually outgrow the simplicity of a pure‑text note app, so the recommendation leans toward the flexible side.

2. **Map required features** – List the must‑have functions (e.g., offline editing, file attachments, tagging, version history).  
3. **Score each candidate** – Use a 1‑5 scale for each feature, then weight the scores according to importance.  
4. **Calculate a total** – Multiply scores by weights, sum them, and compare totals.

## Real‑world scenarios and why Notion wins  

### 1. University student building a research repository  

A student often needs to store lecture notes, PDFs of journal articles, and citation metadata. Notion’s **database tables** let you create a “Reading List” where each entry can hold a title, author, PDF attachment, and tags for subject area. Linked views (e.g., a kanban board for “To read / In progress / Completed”) keep the workflow visual.  

*Why Notion beats Evernote here*: Evernote’s tagging system is flat; you cannot create relational links between notes without third‑party tools. OneNote’s section hierarchy can become unwieldy when you have hundreds of articles.  

### 2. Small business tracking client meetings  

A sales team may need to capture meeting notes, attach contracts, and share them with a manager. Notion’s **shared pages with granular permissions** let you give a client view‑only access to a specific page while the whole team retains edit rights. The built‑in **comments** replace the need for a separate chat thread.  

*Why Notion beats Apple Notes*: Apple Notes does not support per‑page permissions or comments, making it unsuitable for client‑facing documentation.  

### 3. Freelance writer organising ideas  

A writer often jots down story ideas, outlines, and research snippets. Notion’s **template button** can generate a pre‑filled page structure (title, logline, character sheet) with a single click, ensuring consistency across dozens of drafts.  

*Why Notion beats OneNote*: OneNote’s page templates are static and limited to the Office ecosystem; Notion’s templates are fully customisable and can be duplicated instantly.  

### 4. Casual user needing quick capture  

If you only need to capture a shopping list or a fleeting thought, opening Notion can feel heavy. Keeping **Apple Notes** (iOS/macOS) or **Google Keep** (Android) as a secondary capture tool lets you record instantly, then later copy the content into Notion for longer‑term organisation.  

*Why a backup app matters*: Notion’s web interface may be slower on low‑bandwidth connections, whereas native note apps are optimised for speed.

## How to verify the recommendation for yourself  

1. **Create a free Notion account** – The free tier imposes a 5 MB file‑upload limit per file and a 1 GB total storage cap. For most text‑heavy use, this is sufficient.  
2. **Set up a test database** – Import a small CSV of your recent notes (e.g., a list of meeting titles). Observe how quickly you can filter, sort, and link entries.  
3. **Test sync latency** – Add a note on your phone, then open Notion on your laptop within a minute. If the change appears instantly, the sync meets expectations.  
4. **Check offline behaviour** – Switch your device to airplane mode, edit a page, then reconnect. Notion should reconcile changes without data loss.  
5. **Compare export** – In Notion, select “Export → Markdown & CSV”. Open the files in a text editor to confirm formatting is retained. Do the same in Evernote (ENEX) and note any extra steps required to convert to a usable format.  

If any step feels unsatisfactory—e.g., sync takes more than a few minutes, or the learning curve feels prohibitive—re‑evaluate the weighting in the decision framework. You may discover that a simpler app scores higher for your specific context.

## What to do about it  

1. **Adopt Notion as your primary note‑taking platform** – Sign up for the free tier, import existing notes, and build a simple “Inbox” page to capture everything at first.  
2. **Create a quick‑capture backup** – Enable Apple Notes (iOS/macOS) or Google Keep (Android) for on‑the‑go entries; set a weekly reminder to migrate anything worth keeping into Notion.  
3. **Invest a few hours in template building** – Use Notion’s built‑in template gallery or community‑shared templates for meetings, research, and writing. Tailor them to your workflow to reduce the learning curve.  
4. **Review quarterly** – Re‑run the decision framework every three months. If your needs have shifted (e.g., you require heavy‑duty PDF annotation), consider adding a specialised tool alongside Notion rather than replacing it.  

By committing to Notion now, you secure a scalable, cross‑device system that can accommodate both quick notes and complex knowledge bases, while retaining a lightweight fallback for the moments when speed matters more than structure.
