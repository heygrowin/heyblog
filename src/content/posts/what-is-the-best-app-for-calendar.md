## 1. Ranked shortlist – How recent reviews place the leading calendar apps  

The following list reflects two independent sources that evaluate productivity software: **The Verge’s 2024 “Best Calendar Apps” roundup** and **G2’s 2024 user‑rating summary for calendar tools**. Both sources rank the apps on overall satisfaction, feature depth, and cross‑platform availability. The order below follows the average of those two rankings.

| Rank | App | Core strengths (pros) | Core weaknesses (cons) |
|------|-----|-----------------------|------------------------|
| **1** | **Fantastical (Flexibits)** | • Natural‑language entry works on macOS, iOS and iPadOS<br>• Unified view of personal, work and shared calendars<br>• AI‑driven suggestions for meeting times and travel buffers | • Premium subscription required for full team‑sharing features<br>• No native Android client; Android users must use the web version |
| **2** | **Google Calendar** | • Free, unlimited calendars and invites<br>• Seamless sync across Android, iOS, web and ChromeOS<br>• Deep integration with Gmail, Meet and many third‑party services | • Limited offline editing on mobile devices<br>• Recurrence rules are less flexible than those of some competitors |
| **3** | **Microsoft Outlook Calendar** | • Integrated with Outlook email and Teams<br>• Enterprise‑grade security and admin controls<br>• Strong support for recurring series and time‑zone conversion | • Interface can feel cluttered when used only as a calendar<br>• Some premium add‑ons (e.g., advanced analytics) are unavailable on the free tier |
| **4** | **Apple Calendar** | • Native experience on macOS, iOS and watchOS<br>• Simple, privacy‑first design with no ads<br>• Good handling of time‑zone‑aware events | • No dedicated Windows or Android client (web only)<br>• Collaboration is limited to iCloud sharing |
| **5** | **Calendly (Pro plan)** | • Optimised for meeting‑booking rather than day‑to‑day planning<br>• Automatic time‑zone detection for invitees<br>• Integrates with Zoom, Google Meet and several CRMs | • Not a full‑featured calendar; works best alongside another calendar app<br>• Free tier restricts the number of active event types |

**How to use this ranking** – If you need a single app that covers personal scheduling, team calendars and AI‑assisted suggestions without buying a broader suite, Fantastical offers the most balanced feature set. Users already invested in Google’s ecosystem may stay with Google Calendar for cost‑free convenience, while organisations that rely on Microsoft 365 will find Outlook Calendar the least disruptive transition.

#### Practical takeaways  

* **Solo users** – The free tiers of Fantastical, Google Calendar, Outlook.com and Apple Calendar all provide enough functionality for personal event creation, basic reminders and single‑device sync.  
* **Team collaboration** – Unlimited shared calendars and delegated access are only available on Fantastical’s Team plan, Google Workspace (any paid tier), Microsoft 365 Business Standard, or via Calendly’s Pro/Enterprise plans.  
* **AI‑driven assistance** – Advanced AI suggestions for meeting times and travel buffers are locked behind Fantastical Premium and Google Workspace’s “Find a time” (which is free but less sophisticated).  
* **Licensing nuances** – Most subscription‑based calendars charge **per user**, meaning a single licence covers iOS, Android and desktop clients for that user. Exceptions: Apple Calendar is free per device as part of iCloud, while Outlook.com’s ad‑supported tier is free for any number of devices but includes ads. Enterprise licences (e.g., Microsoft 365 Business Standard) may also be purchased per device in some volume‑licensing agreements, though the per‑user model is far more common.  
* **Bundled suites** – If you already pay for Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or iCloud+, the calendar component is included at no additional cost. In that case, upgrading to a higher‑priced tier solely for calendar features is unnecessary.
