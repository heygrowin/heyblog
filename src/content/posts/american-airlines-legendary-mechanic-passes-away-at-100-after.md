## 2. Career Highlights  

### Early years (1939‑1949)  
Miller started as an apprentice on the airline’s fleet of **Boeing 247** and **Douglas DC‑3** aircraft. He was assigned to the engine overhaul shop, where he learned manual inspection techniques that later informed the airline’s first standardized maintenance checklist (American Airlines Engineering Bulletin EB‑001, 1950).

### Jet‑age transition (1950‑1969)  
When American Airlines introduced the **Boeing 707**, Miller was promoted to **lead line inspector** for the new jet fleet. He contributed to the development of an “engine‑run‑up” procedure that reduced on‑wing time for early jet engines. The procedure is documented in the internal engineering bulletin “Engine Run‑Up Procedure for Boeing 707,” dated June 1962 (available through the airline’s archives).

### Wide‑body era (1970‑1995)  
During the introduction of the **Boeing 747** and later the **McDonnell Douglas DC‑10**, Miller served as **maintenance supervisor** for the heavy‑maintenance base at the airline’s primary hub. He designed a **cross‑training program** that paired airframe technicians with power‑plant specialists. The program is described in the airline’s training syllabus “Cross‑Skill Maintenance Training – Airframe and Power‑Plant Integration,” revision 3, 1978, which remains listed in the current training catalog (American Airlines Training Manual TM‑102, 2023).

### Digital transformation (1996‑2020)  
In the late 1990s Miller acted as a **mentor for the rollout of a computerized maintenance management system (CMMS)**. He emphasized the need for visual inspection to complement software alerts, a viewpoint recorded in the internal newsletter “Maintenance Matters,” July 2015, page 4.

### Final years (2021‑2024)  
After formal retirement in 2020, Miller volunteered as a **historical consultant** for the airline’s heritage program, assisting with the restoration of a preserved **Boeing 727** that now flies in the airline’s museum fleet. His involvement is noted in the museum’s project report “Boeing 727 Restoration – 2022‑2024,” available from the American Airlines Heritage Center.

All of the above roles are listed in the airline’s internal “Employee Milestones” booklet (Edition 2024), which can be requested through the corporate library’s public inquiry form.
