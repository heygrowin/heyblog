Buy a **HEPA‑plus‑activated‑carbon air purifier with a Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) of at least 300 m³/h for rooms up to 30 sq m, and choose a model that offers a filter‑life indicator and an Indian after‑sales network**.  
That single decision balances the three things most Indian buyers care about: health impact, running cost, and serviceability. | Factor | What it means for you | Typical Indian impact |

The decision you make today must sit somewhere on this four‑point matrix. A unit that scores high on performance but is expensive to run may be unaffordable in the long run, while a cheap unit that never reaches the required CADR will give a false sense of safety.

## What you really need to measure before you buy

### Checking your indoor air quality

1. **Use a low‑cost sensor** – devices such as the **Xiaomi Mi Home Air Detector** (≈ ₹2,500) or the **Awair Element** (≈ ₹7,000) give real‑time PM2.5, PM10 and VOC readings.  
2. **Cross‑check with government data** – the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) publishes city‑wide PM2.5 averages on its website; many state pollution boards also have mobile apps.  
3. **Identify the peak concentration** – note the highest PM2.5 value you see over a week. If it regularly exceeds 150 µg/m³, you need a purifier that can handle heavy loads.

If you cannot afford a sensor, a simple rule of thumb works for most Indian homes: **any room that faces a busy road, a kitchen that uses solid‑fuel cooking, or a bedroom on the ground floor in a polluted city should be treated as “high‑pollution”** and matched with the recommendation above.

## Filter technology and why it matters

### HEPA vs non‑HEPA filters

- **HEPA (High Efficiency Particulate Air)** – captures 99.97 % of particles ≥ 0.3 µm, which includes most PM2.5, pollen, dust mite debris and bacterial spores.  
- **Activated carbon** – adsorbs gases and odours such as cooking fumes, cigarette smoke, and formaldehyde.  
- **Ionisers and Ozone generators** – claim to “clean” air by charging particles, but in practice they can produce ozone levels above the safe limit set by the Indian Ministry of Environment (0.05 ppm). Use only if the product explicitly states “ozone‑free”.  
- **UV‑C lamps** – target microbes but do not affect particulate matter; they add to electricity use and require regular lamp replacement.

For Indian conditions, **HEPA plus activated carbon is the only combination that reliably tackles both the particulate pollution from traffic and the VOCs from indoor cooking**. Anything less will leave a measurable gap in protection.

## Sizing the unit correctly

The CADR tells you how many cubic metres of clean air the purifier can deliver per hour. To avoid under‑sizing, calculate the required CADR for your room:

```
Required CADR (m³/h) = Room Volume (m³) × Desired Air Changes per Hour (ACH)
```

- **Room volume** = length × breadth × height (most Indian homes have ceiling heights of 2.8 m).  
- **ACH** – a minimum of 4 ACH is recommended for polluted environments; 6 ACH is ideal for allergy sufferers.

### Example calculation

A bedroom measuring 4 m × 3 m × 2.8 m has a volume of 33.6 m³.  
Desired ACH = 4 → Required CADR = 33.6 × 4 ≈ 135 m³/h.

Because you also want a safety margin for peak outdoor pollution, **round up to the next standard CADR rating, typically 150 m³/h or higher**. For a living‑room of 6 m × 5 m × 2.8 m (84 m³), the calculation gives 84 × 4 = 336 m³/h, which aligns with the 300 + CADR recommendation.

| **Service** | Warranty 2 years, extended service plans ₹1,000 – ₹2,000 per year for brands with Indian service centres. | Verify service‑centre locations on the brand’s “Support” page. |

The **total cost of ownership over three years** can be estimated by adding purchase price, three filter replacements, and electricity. For a ₹10,000 purifier with ₹3,500 filters replaced twice a year, the three‑year total is roughly ₹10,000 + (₹3,500 × 6) + (₹12 × 36) ≈ ₹31,000. Use this figure to compare against higher‑priced models; the difference is often justified only by a substantially higher CADR or additional smart features.

## What to do about it

1. **Measure your indoor PM2.5** for at least three days using a cheap sensor or a government app. Note the highest value.  
2. **Calculate the required CADR** with the room‑volume formula; add a 20 % safety margin.  
3. **Pick a HEPA‑plus‑activated‑carbon unit** that meets or exceeds that CADR, preferably the Philips AC2887/30 if it is available in your city.  
4. **Verify filter‑life and price** on the retailer’s page; note the part number for future replacements.  
5. **Place the order** through a trusted Indian e‑commerce platform or a local electronics store that offers a warranty and service centre.  
6. **Set up the filter‑life reminder** (most units have a built‑in LED or a smartphone notification) so you replace the filter on schedule.  

By following these steps you will have a purifier that actually reduces the particulate and odour load in your home, costs a predictable amount to run, and can be serviced without importing spare parts. The decision is clear: choose a properly sized HEPA‑carbon unit now, and protect your indoor air for the months ahead.
