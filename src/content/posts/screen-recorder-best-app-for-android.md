## 1️⃣ Best video‑quality & resolution options – which app tops the chart?

Screen‑recording apps on Android rely on the device’s hardware encoder (MediaCodec) when it is available. The encoder determines the highest resolution and frame rate that can be captured without over‑loading the CPU.

### 1.2 How to lock the highest quality in XRecorder  

1. **Install and open XRecorder** – grant the “Display over other apps” and “Storage” permissions when prompted.  
2. Tap the **gear icon** (Settings) → **Video**.  
3. **Resolution:** choose **3840 × 2160 (4K)**. If the option is greyed out, the device’s hardware encoder does not support 4K and the app will automatically switch to 1080p.  
4. **Frame rate:** select **60 fps**.  
5. **Bitrate:** a setting of **30 Mbps** provides a good balance between visual quality and file size; increase to **45 Mbps** for the cleanest image if storage is not a concern.  
6. **Encoder:** keep **Hardware (MediaCodec)** selected. Switching to software encoding raises CPU load dramatically and may cause dropped frames.  
7. **HDR:** on Android 12 or newer, enable the **HDR** toggle. The app will embed HDR metadata only when the source content is HDR.  
8. **Audio source:** choose **Internal + Mic** if you need both streams; otherwise pick the preferred source.  
9. Press **Back** to save the settings, then start a recording using the floating control.  
10. After stopping, open the file in the **Gallery** → **Details** to verify the recorded resolution, frame rate, and bitrate.

## 2️⃣ Free vs. paid – can you get watermark‑free, editing‑ready recordings at zero cost?

Many users look for a completely free solution that still offers a usable editor and no watermarks. The reality varies by app:

* **ADV Screen Recorder** – The free version records without a visible watermark and includes a basic trim editor. Higher‑bitrate options and removal of ads require the Pro upgrade.  
* **Screen Recorder – No Ads** – As the name suggests, this app does not display ads and does not add a watermark, but it caps recording at 1080p / 30 fps and lacks advanced editing tools.  
* **XRecorder** – The free tier adds a small logo watermark; removing it and unlocking 4K / 60 fps requires the paid subscription.  
* **AZ Screen Recorder** – The free version places a watermark; the Premium subscription removes it and adds features such as picture‑in‑picture (PiP) mode and higher bitrate control.  
* **RecMe** – Free recordings are watermark‑free, but the app limits some export options and internal‑audio capture to the paid tier.

### 2.2 Using the free tiers responsibly  

All of the apps listed above provide a legitimate free tier that complies with the developers’ licensing terms. The typical limitations are:

* **Watermarks** – Most free versions add a small on‑screen logo. If a watermark‑free video is required, the paid upgrade is the only supported method.  
* **Bitrate and resolution caps** – Free tiers usually stop at 1080p / 30 fps (or 1080p / 60 fps with a modest bitrate). Higher bitrate or 4K recording needs a hardware encoder that the app can access, which is only unlocked in the paid version for XRecorder and some others.  
* **Ads** – Free versions may display banner or interstitial ads. These do not affect the recorded video but can be distracting when navigating the UI.  

When a free app meets your needs (e.g., watermark‑free 1080p capture with basic trimming), there is no need to employ unofficial workarounds. All documented features are described in each app’s help section or Play Store listing.
