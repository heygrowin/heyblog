If you need a note‑taking app for iPad **today**, the most reliable choice is **GoodNotes 5**. It balances handwriting fidelity, organisational tools, and cross‑device sync without the subscription‑only model that drives many competitors. The decision hinges on three trade‑offs: how much you value natural‑looking ink, how you organise large volumes of notes, and whether you are prepared to pay a one‑off fee rather than a recurring charge.

## Core decision: why GoodNotes 5 beats the alternatives

GoodNotes 5 delivers the closest experience to writing on paper, thanks to its vector‑based ink engine that adapts to the Apple Pencil’s tilt and pressure. It also offers a folder hierarchy that mirrors a physical filing cabinet, making it easy to locate notes after months of use. Most importantly, the app is a **single purchase (£9.99)** on the App Store, so you avoid the ongoing costs that Notability (£11.99 per year) and Microsoft OneNote (free but tied to a Microsoft 365 subscription for full features) impose.

If you are comfortable paying once and keeping the app forever, GoodNotes 5 gives you:

* **Consistent handwriting rendering** – the ink stays crisp at any zoom level, and the built‑in shape recogniser turns rough sketches into clean diagrams.
* **Robust organisation** – unlimited notebooks, nested folders, and a powerful search that indexes handwritten text, typed text, and PDF annotations.
* **Full‑featured export** – PDF, PNG, and GoodNotes‑specific formats can be shared via AirDrop, email, or cloud services without losing layout.
* **Offline‑first design** – notes are stored locally first, then synced via iCloud; you can work without Wi‑Fi and the data will reconcile later.

The alternatives each have a niche advantage, but they also introduce friction that most users will encounter quickly.

## Key criteria to weigh

Before confirming the decision, list the factors that matter to you. Use the checklist below to see where GoodNotes 5 aligns with your priorities.

1. **Handwriting quality** – does the app capture pressure and tilt accurately?
2. **Searchability** – can you find a word you wrote months ago?
3. **Organisation** – are folders, tags, or notebooks sufficient for your workflow?
4. **Export options** – do you need PDFs, images, or a proprietary format?
5. **Cost model** – one‑off purchase versus subscription?
6. **Cross‑platform sync** – do you need Windows or Android access?
7. **Audio recording** – is attaching voice notes a requirement?
8. **Learning curve** – how long before you feel productive?

Mark each criterion with a tick if it is essential, a dash if it is optional, and leave blank if it does not matter. The more essential items you have, the more weight you should give to the app that satisfies them without compromise.

### GoodNotes 5 – strengths and limits

*Strengths* – The app’s ink engine is the most mature on iPad, and the search index works reliably for both typed and handwritten notes. Its folder system lets you create a deep hierarchy without performance slowdown.

*Limits* – It does not support audio recordings, which some students and journalists find essential. It also lacks a native Android client, so you must rely on iCloud web access if you need a non‑Apple device.

### Notability – strengths and limits

*Strengths* – Audio sync is seamless: the app records while you write and later lets you tap any point in the audio to hear what you said at that moment. Its interface is simple and the subscription includes regular feature updates.

*Limits* – The subscription model means you will pay indefinitely. Search for handwritten text is slower and sometimes misses words, especially if the ink is light. Folder organisation is flatter, which can become unwieldy with many notebooks.

### Apple Notes – strengths and limits

*Strengths* – Free and pre‑installed, with tight integration into iOS. Handwriting is decent for casual use, and the app now supports scanning documents directly into a note.

*Limits* – Search does not index handwriting, and there is no robust folder hierarchy beyond simple folders. Export options are limited to PDF, and the app does not support complex page templates.

### OneNote – strengths and limits

*Strengths* – Free cross‑platform sync, including Windows and Android. Handwritten search works via Microsoft’s OCR engine, and you can embed audio, video, and Office files.

*Limits* – The ink engine feels less natural than GoodNotes 5, especially on the Apple Pencil. The UI is geared toward a “digital notebook” concept that can feel cluttered on an iPad screen. Full feature set requires a Microsoft 365 subscription for business users.

### How to verify the scores yourself

1. **Handwriting test** – Open each app, write a paragraph using the Apple Pencil, then zoom in to 200 % and check for jagged edges or pixelation. Note the visual quality.
2. **Search test** – Write a unique word (e.g., “zebra‑quark”) in each app, close it, then use the built‑in search. Record whether the word appears.
3. **Export test** – Export a page as PDF from each app and open it in a third‑party viewer (e.g., Adobe Acrobat). Confirm that formatting and ink remain intact.
4. **Cost verification** – Visit the App Store page for each app and note the listed price or subscription terms. If a trial is offered, sign up and note the expiry date.
5. **Sync check** – Enable iCloud (or the relevant cloud service) on two iPads, create a note on one, and confirm it appears on the other within a few minutes.

By performing these quick checks, you can replace the qualitative “high/medium/low” labels with personal observations that feed directly into the scorecard.

## Practical steps to implement the decision today

1. **Purchase GoodNotes 5** – Open the App Store on your iPad, search “GoodNotes 5”, and tap the £9.99 purchase button. The transaction is a one‑off charge; no further subscription is required.
2. **Set up iCloud sync** – In Settings → [Your Name] → iCloud, toggle GoodNotes 5 on. This ensures your notebooks are backed up and available on any other Apple device you own.
3. **Create a folder structure** – Start with top‑level folders such as “Work”, “Study”, “Personal”. Inside each, add sub‑folders for projects or subjects. GoodNotes 5 lets you drag and drop notebooks to reorganise them quickly.
4. **Import existing PDFs** – If you have lecture slides or reports, tap the “+” button, choose “Import”, and select the files from Files or your email. GoodNotes 5 will treat each page as a canvas you can annotate.
5. **Enable handwriting search** – Go to Settings → GoodNotes → Search and ensure “Handwriting” is turned on. The first time you search, the app will index all existing notes; this may take a few minutes.
6. **Test export** – Open a notebook, tap the share icon, choose “Export as PDF”, and send it to yourself via email. Open the PDF on a non‑Apple device to confirm the layout is preserved.
7. **Schedule a weekly review** – Set a recurring calendar event to tidy your folders and tag any notes that need follow‑up. This habit prevents the system from becoming cluttered over time.

If any of the steps reveal a missing feature you cannot live without (e.g., audio recording), revisit the decision framework and consider whether a secondary app like Notability for audio‑heavy sessions is warranted. However, for the majority of note‑taking scenarios—meeting minutes, lecture notes, project brainstorming—GoodNotes 5 will meet the core requirements without additional cost.
