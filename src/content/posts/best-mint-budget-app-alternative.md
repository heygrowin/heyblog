Mint remains a popular free budgeting tool, but it trades strong feature set for a data‑collection model that many users find uncomfortable. The core decision is therefore **privacy versus convenience**: you can keep Mint for its automatic transaction syncing and visual dashboards, or you can move to an alternative that stores data locally or uses a subscription model that limits data sharing. The best Mint budget‑app alternative will depend on which side of that trade‑off you sit, and on how much you value things like multi‑currency support, investment tracking, and custom categorisation.

## What to look for in a Mint replacement  

Before naming specific apps, it helps to set out the criteria that matter most to most UK users. The list below captures the most common requirements; you can rank them for yourself to see which apps align with your priorities.

- **Data privacy and storage** – Does the app keep your data on‑device, in an encrypted cloud, or on a server that may be used for advertising?
- **Bank feed integration** – How many UK banks can the app automatically import transactions from, and how reliable is the sync?
- **Cost structure** – Is the app truly free, freemium, or subscription‑based? Are there hidden fees for premium features?
- **Feature breadth** – Does it cover budgeting, bill reminders, goal tracking, and investment performance in one place?
- **User‑interface simplicity** – Is the layout intuitive for occasional users, or does it require a steep learning curve?
- **Export options** – Can you download CSV or OFX files for further analysis or backup?
- **Cross‑platform availability** – Are iOS, Android, web, and desktop versions all supported?

You can turn this checklist into a simple scoring sheet: give each criterion a weight from 1 (low importance) to 5 (high importance), then rate each app on a 1‑5 scale. Multiplying the weight by the rating and adding the totals will give you a personalised ranking.

## Top Mint alternatives for the UK market  

### 1. YNAB (You Need A Budget)  

**Privacy** – YNAB stores data in an encrypted cloud but does not sell it to third parties. The company’s privacy policy is publicly available and can be inspected for any changes.  

**Bank feeds** – Direct import works with most major UK banks via Plaid. If a bank is not supported, you can upload CSV statements manually.  

**Cost** – £11.99 per month or £84 per year (prices checked on the YNAB website in August 2026). A 34‑day free trial is offered; no permanent free tier exists.  

**Features** – Strong focus on zero‑based budgeting, real‑time goal tracking, and debt‑pay‑off plans. Investment tracking is limited to manual entry, so it is less suitable for users with large portfolios.  

**Interface** – Clean, colour‑coded envelopes make it easy to see where money is allocated. The learning curve is moderate because the methodology differs from Mint’s “spending categories”.  

**Export** – CSV export of all transactions is available, as is a JSON API for advanced users.  

### 2. MoneyDashboard  

**Privacy** – Operates under a UK‑based data‑protection framework and does not use data for advertising.  

**Bank feeds** – Directly connects to most UK banks using the Open Banking API, which provides read‑only access without storing login credentials.  

**Cost** – Free for core budgeting features. A “Premium” tier (£4.99 per month) adds custom categories, advanced reporting, and priority support.  

**Features** – Automatic categorisation, cash‑flow forecasts, and a visual “spending wheel”. Investment accounts can be linked, but performance charts are basic.  

**Interface** – Dashboard‑centric, with a strong emphasis on visualising trends over time. Navigation is straightforward for users accustomed to Mint.  

**Export** – Allows CSV download of transactions and budgets; no native OFX export.  

### 3. Emma (formerly Emma Money)  

**Privacy** – Stores data in encrypted servers located in the EU. The company states it does not sell personal data, and it complies with GDPR.  

**Bank feeds** – Supports over 300 banks worldwide, including most UK banks via Plaid and Open Banking.  

**Cost** – Free tier includes basic budgeting and subscription tracking. Premium (£6.99 per month) unlocks cash‑flow predictions, investment analysis, and priority support.  

**Features** – In addition to budgeting, Emma highlights recurring subscriptions, helping users cancel unwanted services. It also offers a “spending insights” AI that suggests savings opportunities.  

**Interface** – Modern, card‑based layout that mirrors a financial dashboard. Some users find the colour scheme busy, but the navigation is shallow enough for quick checks.  

**Export** – CSV export for transactions; no direct integration with accounting software.  

### 4. PocketSmith  

**Privacy** – Stores data on secure UK servers; offers a “self‑hosted” option for organisations that need on‑premise storage.  

**Bank feeds** – Connects to UK banks via Open Banking, and also supports manual CSV import for overseas accounts.  

**Cost** – Free tier limited to 10 accounts and basic budgeting. Paid plans start at £9.99 per month (Personal) and £19.99 per month (Premium), with annual discounts.  

**Features** – Unique “future cash‑flow projection” that lets you model scenarios (e.g., a salary raise or a new mortgage). Investment tracking includes portfolio performance and dividend forecasts.  

**Interface** – Timeline view is distinctive; it can be overwhelming at first but provides a granular view of cash movement.  

**Export** – CSV and OFX exports are supported; there is also an API for developers.

### Decision framework  

Below is a simple flow‑chart‑style list you can follow to narrow the field:

1. **Do you need a free app?**  
   - **Yes** → Go to MoneyDashboard (free core) or PocketSmith (free limited).  
   - **No** → Continue.

2. **Is investment performance a priority?**  
   - **Yes** → PocketSmith (detailed) or Emma (basic).  
   - **No** → YNAB or MoneyDashboard.

3. **Do you prefer a methodology that forces every pound to have a job?**  
   - **Yes** → YNAB (zero‑based).  
   - **No** → MoneyDashboard or Emma.

4. **Is data residency (UK servers) a hard requirement?**  
   - **Yes** → MoneyDashboard or PocketSmith.  
   - **No** → YNAB or Emma.

5. **Are you comfortable paying a subscription for premium features?**  
   - **Yes** → Choose the highest‑rated option from the previous steps.  
   - **No** → Stick with the free tier of MoneyDashboard or PocketSmith.

By answering each question, you can eliminate at least two of the four apps and focus on the one that matches your priorities.

## How to migrate from Mint to your chosen app  

1. **Export your Mint data** – In Mint, go to *Settings → Export data* and download the CSV file. This file contains all transactions, categories, and budgets up to the export date.  
2. **Create a backup** – Store the CSV on an encrypted drive or a password‑protected cloud folder. This ensures you have a reference point if any import errors occur.  
3. **Import into the new app** – Most alternatives have an “Import CSV” option in the settings or budgeting section. Follow the on‑screen mapping prompts; you may need to match Mint’s column headings (Date, Description, Amount, Category) to the app’s fields.  
4. **Re‑categorise where needed** – Automatic categorisation is rarely perfect. Spend a short session reviewing the first month of imported data and correcting any mismatches.  
5. **Reconnect bank feeds** – Log into the new app and authorise each bank connection. If the app uses Open Banking, you will be redirected to your bank’s login page; no credentials are stored by the budgeting service.  
6. **Set up goals and alerts** – Replicate any savings goals, bill reminders, or low‑balance alerts you previously used in Mint. Most apps allow you to copy a goal template, which speeds the process.  
7. **Test for a month** – Run both Mint and the new app side‑by‑side for a few weeks. Compare totals, check that recurring transactions appear as expected, and verify that alerts fire at the right thresholds.  

If you encounter discrepancies, revisit the CSV import mapping or check whether the new app’s categorisation rules differ from Mint’s. Most support forums provide step‑by‑step guides for common import issues.

## What to do about it  

If privacy is your primary concern and you are comfortable paying a modest subscription, **YNAB** offers a clear, methodical budgeting system with strong encryption and no advertising. For users who need a free, UK‑focused solution and are happy with visual dashboards, **MoneyDashboard** provides solid bank‑feed integration and a transparent data policy. If you want a blend of subscription‑cancellation insights and a modest premium price, **Emma** is worth testing during its free tier. Finally, if you require detailed cash‑flow forecasts and investment tracking, **PocketSmith** delivers those features, albeit with a higher price point.

Pick the app that scores highest against the criteria you value most, follow the migration steps above, and give yourself a month to confirm the fit. After that period, you’ll know whether the trade‑off you accepted—privacy versus convenience—has been worth the change.
