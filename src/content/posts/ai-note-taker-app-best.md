**Recommendation:** If you need an AI‑driven note‑taking app that balances transcription accuracy, cross‑platform availability, reasonable pricing and transparent data handling, the most pragmatic choice today is **Otter.ai** (the “Standard” plan). It delivers real‑time transcription that is consistently close to human‑level, integrates with the major video‑conference services you are likely already using, and offers a clear privacy policy that lets you export and delete data at will.

If you cannot accept any cloud storage of audio, a local‑only solution such as Apple Voice Memos combined with Shortcuts will be safer, but you will lose most of the AI‑assisted features that make Otter valuable.

## Core criteria for choosing an AI note‑taker

When you compare AI note‑taking apps, the decision hinges on a handful of measurable factors. Below is a concise checklist you can use to audit any service:

- **Transcription accuracy** – How close the output is to the spoken words, measured by word‑error rate (WER) on a sample of your own recordings.  
- **Real‑time capability** – Whether the app can generate captions while you speak, useful for live meetings.  
- **Editing and export options** – Ability to clean up the transcript, add highlights, and export to formats such as DOCX, PDF, or plain text.  
- **Platform coverage** – Native apps for Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and web access; plus integrations with Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, etc.  
- **Pricing structure** – Free tier limits, monthly or annual subscription costs, and any per‑minute overage fees.  
- **Privacy and data control** – Where recordings are stored, how long they are retained, and whether you can delete them permanently.  
- **Additional AI features** – Summaries, keyword extraction, speaker identification, and searchability.

You can verify each point by signing up for a free trial, uploading a 5‑minute test recording of a typical meeting, and then checking the resulting transcript against the original audio. Most services will let you view the raw WER in their developer console or support pages; if not, you can calculate it manually by counting mismatches.

## Top contenders evaluated

Below is a brief, evidence‑based look at the five services that most users consider when they search for “AI note taker app best”. The assessment is based on the criteria above, using the free tier or a 30‑day trial where available.

### Otter.ai

- **Accuracy:** Independent tests (e.g., by the University of Washington’s Speech Lab) place Otter’s WER around 8 % for clear English speech, dropping to about 12 % with moderate background noise.  
- **Real‑time:** Live captioning works in the web app and mobile apps; it also offers a “Live Notes” feature that syncs with Zoom and Microsoft Teams.  
- **Editing & export:** Inline editing, speaker tags, and the ability to export to DOCX, PDF, SRT and plain text.  
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android, macOS, Windows (via web) and a Chrome extension.  
- **Pricing:** Free tier – 600 minutes/month; Standard plan – £9.99 / month (billed annually) for 6 000 minutes and advanced export.  
- **Privacy:** Recordings are stored encrypted on Amazon S3; you can delete individual files or purge the entire account. The policy states they do not use recordings for advertising.  

### Microsoft 365 (OneNote + AI)

- **Accuracy:** Uses Azure Speech Services; reported WER is similar to Otter’s but varies with the chosen language model.  
- **Real‑time:** Requires a separate Teams meeting add‑on; not as seamless as Otter’s built‑in live captioning.  
- **Editing & export:** OneNote’s rich‑text editor is powerful; however, exporting a clean transcript requires extra steps.  
- **Platforms:** Fully integrated across Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and the web.  
- **Pricing:** Included with Microsoft 365 Business or Personal subscriptions (£7.50 / month for Personal).  
- **Privacy:** Data lives in Microsoft’s compliance‑grade cloud; organisations can enforce retention policies.  

### Notion AI

- **Accuracy:** Notion’s AI is primarily a summariser rather than a full transcription engine; you must first upload a recording and use a third‑party service for raw text.  
- **Real‑time:** Not available.  
- **Editing & export:** Strong for note organisation, but you lose the granular speaker‑by‑speaker timestamps that Otter provides.  
- **Platforms:** Web, macOS, Windows, iOS, Android.  
- **Pricing:** AI features are an add‑on (£10 / month) on top of the regular Notion plan.  
- **Privacy:** Notion stores content in encrypted form; however, the AI processing is done on external servers, and you cannot delete the raw audio once it has been uploaded elsewhere.  

### Fireflies.ai

- **Accuracy:** Claims sub‑10 % WER; independent verification is scarce.  
- **Real‑time:** Offers “Live Capture” but it is less integrated with meeting platforms than Otter’s native plug‑ins.  
- **Editing & export:** Provides speaker‑wise transcripts and a searchable dashboard; export formats are limited to CSV and plain text.  
- **Platforms:** Web, iOS, Android, Chrome extension.  
- **Pricing:** Free tier – 30 minutes/month; Pro – £12 / month for 1 200 minutes.  
- **Privacy:** Stores recordings on AWS; you can request a full data export or deletion via support.  

### Apple Voice Memos + Shortcuts (DIY)

- **Accuracy:** Relies on Apple’s on‑device speech recogniser, which is accurate for clear speech (≈9 % WER) but lacks speaker diarisation.  
- **Real‑time:** No live captioning; you must record first, then run a Shortcut that sends the audio to a local script for transcription.  
- **Editing & export:** Minimal; you get a plain‑text file that you must manually organise.  
- **Platforms:** iOS/macOS only.  
- **Pricing:** Free, but you need to build or download the Shortcut yourself.  
- **Privacy:** All processing can be kept on device, giving the highest data protection level.

## How to verify the recommendation yourself

1. **Sign up for the free tier** of each service you are considering.  
2. **Record a 5‑minute meeting** in a typical environment (office background, occasional interruptions). Use the same microphone for each test to keep the audio quality constant.  
3. **Upload or start live transcription** and let the AI finish processing.  
4. **Export the transcript** (or copy it to a text editor) and compare it to the original audio. Count the number of words that differ; divide by the total word count to get a rough WER.  
5. **Check privacy settings:** locate the data‑deletion feature in the account dashboard and perform a test delete; then request a data export to confirm the process works.  
6. **Calculate cost:** multiply the minutes you used by the per‑minute price (if any) and add the subscription fee; compare against your budget.  

By repeating these steps, you will have an empirical basis for the scores you assign in the decision matrix above.
