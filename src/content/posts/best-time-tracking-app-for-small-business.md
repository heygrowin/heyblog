The clear decision for most Indian small businesses today is to adopt **Zoho People’s time‑tracking module** (or the integrated Zoho Projects timer) as the primary tool for recording work hours. It balances cost, local support, integration with widely‑used Indian accounting software, and a feature set that scales from a sole‑proprietor to a ten‑person team. Anything cheaper will either lack the compliance‑ready reporting you need for GST and payroll, or will force you to cobble together separate apps that increase admin overhead.

## Why time tracking matters for Indian small businesses

### Compliance and payroll  
Most Indian SMBs still run payroll on spreadsheets or manual punch‑cards. When you need to calculate overtime, factor in the Shops and Establishments Act, or generate Form 16 for employees, an accurate log of hours becomes a legal requirement. A digital tracker automatically timestamps entries, stores them in a tamper‑proof audit trail, and can export data straight into payroll software such as greytHR or Keka.

### Project profitability and client billing  
Freelance designers, boutique IT consultancies, and small manufacturing outfits often bill clients by the hour or by milestone. Without reliable time data you either under‑charge (hurting cash flow) or over‑charge (risking disputes). A built‑in reporting engine lets you see which jobs consume the most labour, where bottlenecks appear, and how to price future work more competitively.

### Remote and hybrid work realities  
Post‑COVID, many Indian firms have adopted a hybrid model. Employees clocking in from home, co‑working spaces, or on‑site need a single source of truth. Zoho People works on Android, iOS, and any web browser, and it stores data on servers located in India, satisfying data‑residency concerns for sectors like fintech or health‑tech.

## Core criteria for a small‑business time‑tracker

When you sit down to choose, evaluate each product against the following checklist. Mark the ones that are “must‑have” for your operation; the rest are “nice‑to‑have”.

- **Local pricing in ₹** – Avoid hidden conversion fees that inflate the cost.
- **GST‑ready invoicing** – Ability to attach time logs to GST‑compliant invoices.
- **Integration with Indian accounting software** – Direct export to Tally ERP 9, Zoho Books, or QuickBooks India.
- **Mobile app with offline mode** – Workers in the field (e.g., delivery, field sales) need to log time without constant internet.
- **Employee self‑service portal** – Allows staff to view their own hours, request corrections, and see leave balances.
- **Admin dashboards & alerts** – Real‑time visibility for owners to spot missed punches or overtime spikes.
- **Data security & Indian data‑center** – Compliance with the Personal Data Protection Bill (when enacted) and RBI guidelines for financial data.
- **Scalability** – Ability to add users without a steep price jump.
- **Customer support in Indian time zones** – Phone or chat that operates during Indian business hours.

## Deep dive into the recommended choice – Zoho People

Zoho publishes its rates on the official website. As of the latest check (April 2024), the **Standard** plan that includes time tracking costs **₹ 250 per user per month** when billed annually, and **₹ 300 per user per month** on a month‑to‑month basis. There is a **Free** tier for up to five users, but it lacks the advanced reporting required for GST invoicing. You can confirm the current price by visiting `zoho.com/people/pricing`.

- **Project management** – Zoho Projects, also part of the Zoho suite, reads the same time entries, so you get a unified view of task progress and cost.

### Data security and support  

Zoho’s Indian data centre is certified under ISO 27001 and complies with the upcoming Personal Data Protection Bill. Support is available via a toll‑free Indian number (1800‑267‑9999) and live chat during standard Indian business hours. The company also offers a 30‑day money‑back guarantee if the product does not meet your expectations.

## Risks and trade‑offs you need to acknowledge

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| **Higher cost than a free app** | ₹ 250 × 10 users = ₹ 2,500 per month, which may strain a very tight cash flow. | Start with the Free tier for up to five users; upgrade only when you need GST‑ready reports. |
| **Learning curve for non‑tech staff** | Some employees may need a few days to become comfortable with the mobile UI. | Use Zoho’s free onboarding webinars and create a short internal cheat‑sheet. |
| **Dependence on internet for real‑time sync** | If your office internet is unreliable, real‑time dashboards may lag. | Enable the offline mode; the app stores entries locally and pushes them when connectivity returns. |
| **Vendor lock‑in** | Moving away from Zoho later could require data migration. | Export all timesheets monthly as CSV; keep a local archive for at least two years (GST audit requirement). |

If your business operates with a single freelancer or a two‑person partnership, a free solution like Clockify may suffice for now, but you will quickly hit limits when you need GST‑compatible invoices or payroll integration.

## How to evaluate before you commit

1. **Sign up for the free trial** – Zoho People offers a 15‑day trial of the Standard plan at no cost.  
2. **Run a pilot with one project** – Choose a client that invoices hourly and use the time‑tracker for two weeks.  
3. **Export the timesheet to Zoho Books** – Verify that the GST fields populate correctly.  
4. **Check mobile offline behaviour** – Take the app to a location with poor 4G (e.g., a rural client site) and log a few entries. Re‑connect later and confirm sync.  
5. **Compare the total admin time** – Measure how many minutes you spend on payroll and invoicing before and after the pilot. If the reduction is significant, the cost is justified.
