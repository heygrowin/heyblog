The core trade‑off when hunting for the cheapest tax‑software is **price versus capability** – the lowest‑cost product will often lack the forms, guidance or support you need, while a more expensive package may include features you will never use. Your task is to match the software’s price to the complexity of your tax situation, the level of assistance you expect, and the platforms you prefer.

## How to map your tax needs to a price bracket  

### 1. Identify the filing complexity  

| Complexity level | Typical forms required | Guidance needed | Typical price range (UK) |
|------------------|------------------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| Simple          | Self‑Assessment (SA100) only | Minimal – basic instructions | Free – £0‑£15 |
| Moderate        | SA100 + supplementary pages (e.g., SA103 for self‑employment, SA108 for capital gains) | Interactive Q&A, error checking | £15‑£30 |
| Complex         | Multiple supplementary pages, partnership returns, trusts, non‑resident filings | Full‑service help, live chat or phone support | £30‑£60+ |

*How to check*: Log in to HMRC’s “Self Assessment” guidance page and list the supplementary pages you will need. Compare that list with the “forms covered” section on each software’s product page.

### 2. Decide on the delivery method  

- **Web‑based only** – No installation, works on any device with a browser. Usually cheaper because the provider does not need to maintain separate desktop versions.  
- **Desktop (Windows/Mac)** – May be required if you need offline access or have a slow internet connection. Often priced a little higher.  
- **Mobile app** – Useful for on‑the‑go data entry, but most apps are tied to the web version and do not affect price substantially.

### 3. Factor in support expectations  

| Support type | Typical cost impact | When it matters |
|--------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Email only | +£0‑£5 | You are comfortable reading help articles and can wait a day for a reply. |
| Live chat | +£5‑£10 | You need quick answers while you’re entering figures. |
| Phone support (business hours) | +£10‑£15 | You prefer speaking to a person, especially for complex queries. |

*How to verify*: Most providers list their support channels on the pricing page. If it’s not clear, use the site’s live chat or contact form to ask “What support is included in the £20 plan?”

## The main tax‑software options in the UK market  

Below is a concise overview of the most widely available products as of mid‑2026. Prices are shown as the base price for a single‑user licence for the 2025‑26 tax year; discounts, promotions or multi‑user licences will change the final amount.

### 1. FreeTax (free tier)  

- **Price**: £0 for simple SA100 returns.  
- **What’s included**: Basic data entry, automatic calculation, e‑filing to HMRC.  
- **Limitations**: No support for supplementary pages, no live chat, no desktop version.  
- **Best for**: Employees with only a single source of income and no capital gains or self‑employment.

### 2. TaxCalc Basic  

- **Price**: £12‑£15 (depending on whether you choose the web or desktop version).  
- **What’s included**: SA100 plus SA103S (self‑employment) and SA108 (capital gains). Email support, step‑by‑step guidance.  
- **Limitations**: No phone support, no partnership or trust returns.  
- **Best for**: Freelancers or sole traders with modest additional income streams.

### 3. ClearTax Pro  

- **Price**: £25 for the web version, £30 for desktop.  
- **What’s included**: Full suite of SA supplementary pages, live chat, and a “review” service where a tax adviser checks your return before submission (additional £10 if you opt in).  
- **Limitations**: Higher price for the same features if you need a desktop licence; the “review” service is optional but adds cost.  
- **Best for**: Small business owners, landlords, or anyone with multiple income sources who want a safety net.

### 4. HMRC‑Approved Partner (e.g., GoSimple)  

- **Price**: £35‑£45 (tiered by the number of supplementary pages).  
- **What’s included**: All forms, phone support during business hours, a “guidance hub” with video tutorials, and a guarantee of error‑free filing (HMRC will reimburse you if a mistake is traced to the software).  
- **Limitations**: The guarantee only applies if you follow the software’s instructions exactly; the price can climb quickly if you need the “premium” version for trusts or overseas income.  
- **Best for**: Professionals handling complex returns, such as accountants preparing returns for multiple clients, or individuals with overseas assets.

### 5. Niche specialist tools (e.g., CryptoTax, PropertyTax)  

- **Price**: Typically £20‑£40, but they focus on a single type of income (cryptocurrency, rental property).  
- **What’s included**: Import of transaction data from exchanges or property management platforms, specialised calculations, limited HMRC filing capability (you still need a main tax software to submit).  
- **Best for**: Users whose primary tax concern is a specialised area and who already have a base tax software.

*How to check current prices*: Visit each provider’s pricing page and look for the “2025‑26 tax year” label. Prices are often displayed in a table that can be filtered by “self‑assessment” or “business”.

## Decision framework: choosing the cheapest software that still meets your needs  

1. **List required forms** – Write down every SA supplementary page you will need.  
2. **Match forms to software** – Use the table above to see which products cover all your forms.  
3. **Add support cost** – If you need phone support, add the relevant surcharge to the base price.  
4. **Calculate total cost** – Multiply by the number of licences (usually one).  
5. **Compare against the “free” threshold** – If the total cost is under £20, you are still in the “budget” range; above that, you are entering the “mid‑tier” or “premium” range.  

**Example**:  
- You are a freelance graphic designer (self‑employment) who also rents a flat (rental income) and has a small portfolio of crypto assets.  
- Required forms: SA100, SA103S, SA105 (property), SA108 (capital gains for crypto).  
- FreeTax is out (no supplementary pages).  
- TaxCalc Basic covers SA103S and SA108 but not SA105 – you would need an add‑on, raising the price to roughly £25.  
- ClearTax Pro includes all four forms, live chat, and optional review – base price £25, plus £10 if you want the review.  
- GoSimple covers all forms, phone support, and the guarantee – price £38.  

In this scenario, **ClearTax Pro** offers the lowest total cost while meeting all functional requirements, assuming you are comfortable with chat support and do not need the guarantee.

## Hidden costs and how to avoid them  

| Hidden cost | Typical source | How to verify | Mitigation |
|-------------|----------------|---------------|------------|
| Upgrade fees for extra forms | Some providers charge per supplementary page | Look for “add‑on” pricing on the product page | Choose a package that already includes all needed pages; if you’re unsure, pick a tier that covers a superset of forms. |
| Late‑submission penalties | Software may not submit before the deadline if you wait too long | Check the provider’s “submission deadline” policy; most state “submission is your responsibility”. | Start the return early and set a personal deadline at least a week before the HMRC deadline. |
| Data‑migration charges | Moving from one software to another may require a paid export | Look for “data export” or “migration” fees in the FAQ | Export your data as a CSV or PDF yourself; most tools allow a manual download at no cost. |
| Subscription renewal at a higher rate | Introductory price may increase next year | Compare the “renewal price” column on the pricing page | Note the renewal price before you buy; if it’s significantly higher, consider a one‑off licence instead of a subscription. |

## Practical steps to confirm the best price today  

1. **Create a shortlist** – Use the table in the “main tax‑software options” section to pick three products that cover all your required forms.  
2. **Visit each website** – Locate the pricing page for the 2025‑26 tax year. Record the base price, any add‑on fees, and support surcharges.  
3. **Check for promotions** – Look for discount codes on reputable coupon sites or on the provider’s own homepage (often a “student discount” or “early‑bird” offer).  
4. **Read recent user reviews** – Sites such as Trustpilot or Which? provide up‑to‑date feedback on reliability and hidden costs.  
5. **Run a cost comparison spreadsheet** – List each product in a column, rows for base price, add‑ons, support, and total. Highlight the lowest total that still meets all criteria.  

## What to do about it  

1. **Map your required SA pages** – Write them down before you start looking at software.  
2. **Pick the cheapest tier that includes every page** – Use the decision framework to add any necessary support fees.  
3. **Verify the total cost on the provider’s site** – Look for hidden add‑on charges and note the renewal price.  
4. **Buy the software, complete your return early** – Starting at least a month before the 31 January deadline gives you time to resolve any issues without penalty.  

If your tax affairs are limited to a single SA100 return, the free tier of a reputable provider will be the best price. For anyone with self‑employment, rental income, or capital gains, a mid‑tier product such as ClearTax Pro (≈£25) usually provides the lowest total cost while still covering all forms and offering live chat support. Choose that, and you’ll stay within a modest budget without sacrificing the essential features you need.
